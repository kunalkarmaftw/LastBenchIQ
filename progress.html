LastBenchIQ

An AI study companion that reads a student's own past exam papers and tells them which topics actually carry the marks — then checks those topics against this year's syllabus, so nothing removed from the course gets revised by mistake.

What the syllabus check caught

Past papers are older than the current syllabus. Matching every question against the 2025 syllabus removed 17 questions worth 87 marks from the ranking.

ISDN appears in four papers and is no longer on the course

Five questions worth 32 marks across 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2019 — including a full 12.5-mark question — ask about ISDN, BRI and PRI reference points. None of it appears anywhere in the 2025 syllabus. A student revising from the paper folder would study all of it and score nothing.

    4

    Data collected

    Every question was read from the original scans by hand and recorded with its marks, year and unit. This is the benchmark the automated pipeline is measured against, and the training set for the question classifier.

    5

    Assumptions tested against real data

    Three design assumptions were measured rather than trusted. Two failed, and finding that out in week one rather than week seven is the point of starting with real papers.

    Failed

    OCR cannot read the marks column

    Tesseract destroys exactly the field the whole ranking depends on, and loses roughly half the question references per page. Vision-based extraction is now the primary path for scans, not a fallback.
    printed OCR read
    (2.5) → @5)
    (5) → ©
    (7.5) → as 7
    Failed

    Similarity scoring cannot judge syllabus membership

    TF-IDF similarity was meant to decide whether a question is still on the syllabus. It measures question length, not membership — the scores for an in-syllabus and a removed topic are indistinguishable. Redesigned into two stages: similarity for topic assignment, term presence for the off-syllabus decision.
    "Write a short note on Modems" in syllabus 0.071
    "Discuss ISDN, its services" removed 0.069
    Held

    The pipeline produces a usable answer

    Extraction → syllabus match → marks aggregation → Pareto banding runs end to end and produces the ranking in section 2, from real papers with no manual tuning per subject.
    Found

    Real uploads are messier than expected

    The AI paper set contained two different courses merged into one file — 41% of those questions belong to a different syllabus. One page was rotated 90° with no rotation recorded in the file. Another subject's pages carried a text layer that looked valid but had its reading order destroyed. All three are now detected rather than silently mis-processed.
